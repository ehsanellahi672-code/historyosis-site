How the differential diagnosis works

Form hypotheses, then look for what supports or excludes them. The panel does this at the bedside for all 76 presenting complaints.

Light or dark? The screens follow this site's theme. Use the Theme button at the top of any page to switch.

A differential that reasons the way you do

The differential diagnosis engine reads the history you tapped, the signs you found, the vitals and the lab and imaging results, and turns them into a prioritised list at the bedside. It follows hypothetico-deductive reasoning: form hypotheses, then look for what supports or excludes each one.

  1. It listens to everything you enterHistory chips, red flags, examination findings, vitals, flagged lab values, risk factors, age and sex.
  2. It never hides the dangerous causes"Must not miss" always comes first and stays visible, even when other findings point away from it.
  3. It tells you what to ask or check next"To tell these apart" suggests the discriminating question, and "Tests that may help" adds a test in one tap.
  4. You stay in chargeOne tap adds a candidate to your differentials, with Undo. Nothing is added or overwritten on its own.

Decision support, not a diagnosis. The engine is rule-based, not AI. It covers all 76 presenting complaints with more than 165 test vignettes. The content is draft and awaiting review by named clinicians. Always apply clinical judgement and local protocols.

How to read the panel

The panel sits in Section E of the record. Each candidate opens to show its reasoning.

Must not miss
Dangerous causes, listed first and always visible in Student and Clinician mode.
Supported by
Only the findings this patient actually has, such as "radiation to the arm or jaw".
Argues against (does not exclude)
Findings that point away. They never remove a candidate.
Not yet known
What you have not asked or examined yet, so you know what is missing.
To tell these apart
The question or examination that best separates the candidates.
Tests that may help
Add a test to the investigations list in one tap.
Student and Clinician mode
Student mode shows the reasoning. Clinician mode shows a compact list.

What it is, and what it is not

Rule-based, not AI

Every rule is written down, cited from standard teaching and tested against vignettes. The same entries always give the same list.

Possibilities, not a diagnosis

Decision support only. It never decides, never blocks saving, and never overwrites what you wrote.

Draft until a clinician signs it off

The content is labelled Draft and awaits review by named clinicians. Use clinical judgement and local protocols.
